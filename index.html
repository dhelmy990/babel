Who owns this memory?

Thinking about lifetimes, ownership, and the cost of a copy.

14 September 2026 · 6 min read

The useful question is often less about where a value lives, and more about who is responsible for its lifetime. Ownership gives that responsibility a name.

A lifetime with a clear ending

A resource should have an owner whose lifetime is easy to follow. When the owner leaves scope, the resource is released. This makes cleanup a property of the program's structure.

auto model = std::make_unique<Model>();
model->predict(input);

// Leaving scope releases the owned model.

In these notes, I find it helpful to sketch the owner separately from the places that merely use the object. An arrow can describe access without implying ownership.

Moving the responsibility

Passing ownership is different from borrowing access. A move makes the transfer visible; a reference lets another part of the program use something without becoming responsible for destroying it.

The habit I want to keep: whenever a lifetime becomes hard to explain, draw the ownership first.
